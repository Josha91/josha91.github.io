What we typically know as _vegetables_ and _fruits_ is mostly a culinary distinction. Biologically speaking, they are all just different parts of plants. 

And that means that, evolutionarily speaking, there are some groupings that you might not expect based on your experience in the kitchen.

For instance, you might expect watermelon and other types of melons, like cantaloupes and honeydew, to be close to each other. But in fact, whereas cantaloupes and honeydew are both of the genus _Cucumis_ and the species _C. melo_, the watermelon is of the genus _Citrullus_.
The _Cucumis_ genus, in fact, includes the cucumber as well. Thus, a cucumber is closer to a honeydew than a watermelon.

In time, watermelons and the _Cucumis_-descendants share their last ancestor 30-40 Myr ago. For cucumbers and honeydew, that is only some 10 Myr.

We can play this game for other fruits and vegetables that are surprisingly related. Tomatoes and potatoes, for instance? Same genus. As well as the eggplant. This is in fact also why they share similar pests and diseases.
