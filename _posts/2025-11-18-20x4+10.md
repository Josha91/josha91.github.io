My wife reminded me the other day of the odd numbering system that the French have between 70 and 99. Most languages I know use a simple base-10 system - that is, 70 is seven-times-ten, 80 is eight-times-ten, etc. But if the French say 90, they say _quatre-vingt-dix_ - four times twenty plus ten. I remembered learning this in high school once upon a time, but what I did not remember is _why_.

## The Vigesimal System
The Vigesimal system is a numeric system, just like the familiar decimal system (base-10), the binary system used primarily in computing (base-2) and the hexadecimal system also frequently used in computing (base-16). The Vigesimal system is a base-20 system. You can use any symbols you like (the Mayans had their own Vigesimal symbols, for instancee), but in Roman alphanumerals the Vigesimal system is often represented by using 0-9 the same way was in the decimal system, and then using A-J for the numbers ten to nineteen. That is, ten would be A, eleven would be B, and so forth.

That means that 10 evaluates to twenty, and 100 evaluates to four hundred, and 1000 evaluates to eight thousand. And DB evaluates to 260 + 11 = 271 decimally.

As a historical tidbit, Abraham Lincoln famously used the Vigesimal System in his Gettysburg Address when he said "_Four score and seven years ago..._".

## Base-20 in French
So, in French the base-20 system is used for the numbers between 70 and 99. Interestingly, most other French speaking regions in the world (including the Democratic Republic of the Congo, French Belgium and French Switzerland) _don't_ use base-20 and use base-10 instead. That is, they say _septante_ to mean 70 rather than _soixante-dix_.

According to the [Wikipedia History of French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_French), the Vigesimal system was introduced twice in French history. First, it was used by pre-Roman Celts. This still has some apparent linguistic links to Welsh, where they use e.g. _deugain_ for 40. However, after the Roman occupation it vanished, until it was re-introduced by Viking invaders who introduced it first to Normany in Northern France, from where it spread further south. Like their Viking forebears, the Danish also still use a Vigesimal system. This North-Germanic root may in fact itself derive from the same Celtic roots that existed in Gaulic France.
