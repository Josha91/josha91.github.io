With a baby that is starting to wean, the topic of _salt_ in her diet comes up a lot. Well-meaning (but frankly overstepping) people tell us all the time to make sure her food is not too salty! And surely we don't give her the food we eat, too! Fine for adults, not for babies, after all. But... where does that advise come from, and is it true?

Indeed, [official recommendations](https://www.nhs.uk/baby/weaning-and-feeding/foods-to-avoid-giving-babies-and-young-children/) say not to give babies too much salt, as their kidneys can't take it. No added salt is the way to go.

But is this based on science? [Lily Nichols, a well-known dietician, says no](https://lilynicholsrdn.com/salt-baby-food-infant-sodium-requirements/). This is further strengthened by [this meta-review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0929693X23001562).

Our takeaway from this: use your intuition. Don't give your baby salty processed food - but not just because of the salt, it's not healthy in general (and not healthy for adults, either!). But salting the water when preparing pasta? Yeah, I don't see the issue with that, eat up junior.
